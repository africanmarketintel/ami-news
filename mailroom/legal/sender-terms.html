AMI Mailroom Terms for senders

Terms for senders

Version 0.1 draft · 14 September 2026

DRAFT. This document has not been reviewed by a lawyer and is not in force. It is published for review only.

In plain terms

You own your list and what you send. ABTA runs the platform and can stop your sending if your mail harms delivery for everyone else.

1. Who we are

AMI Mailroom is operated by African Business Trade Association Limited (company number 16401954), whose registered office is at Fifth Floor, 167–169 Great Portland Street, London, England, W1W 5PF (“ABTA”, “we”, “us”). It is part of African Market Intelligence, an ABTA service.

2. Getting started

You can send as soon as you have created an account, verified your email address and recorded how your list was collected. ABTA does not approve senders in advance.

There is no daily cap. Each account has a monthly allowance, and sending is judged on the complaint and bounce rates of the campaigns you actually send.

Every campaign is checked automatically before it goes. A campaign that appears to ask readers for money, has an image with no description, or comes from an account with no postal address is held until a person at ABTA has looked at it.

3. Your list

You warrant that every address was given to you directly, with consent you can evidence, and that none were bought, scraped, appended or swapped.

You must record for each list how the addresses were collected, what people agreed to, and when. A list without that record cannot be mailed.

You must act on unsubscribes at once, and must not re-add anyone who has left.

4. Your campaigns

Your organisation name and postal address appear in every email, and an unsubscribe link and preference link are added automatically. You must not remove, obscure or break them.

Subject lines must describe the content honestly.

You must not ask a reader for money, bank details or identity documents.

5. Where your email comes from

Unless you set up a domain of your own, your campaigns leave from the shared ABTA sending domain, alongside every other member.

You may instead send from a domain you own, or from a subdomain ABTA sets up for you. You must control the domain you add, and you authorise ABTA and its email provider to send on your behalf from it for as long as it is on your account.

You are responsible for publishing and keeping the DNS records for your own domain. If they are removed, sending from that domain stops.

Sending limits, complaint thresholds and suspension apply to each domain separately. A domain of your own carries your own record, which is the point of it.

6. Shared reputation

On the shared sending domain, your sending affects delivery for every other member.

ABTA sets and may change sending limits, suspends automatically when a campaign passes the complaint or bounce thresholds published on the sending rules page, and may suspend an account or a domain at any time to protect the service. Suspension can happen without notice, because on the shared domain the alternative is every other member losing delivery.

Suspension is not a penalty for its own sake. Tell us what changed and ABTA can reinstate the account or the domain.

7. Data protection

For your subscriber data you are the controller and ABTA is your processor. The data processing terms apply and form part of these terms.

You are responsible for your own privacy notice, for the lawful basis you rely on, and for answering your subscribers’ requests.

8. Liability

ABTA provides the service with reasonable care and skill. It does not promise any rate of delivery, opening or response, and mailbox providers decide where mail lands.

You indemnify ABTA against claims arising from your list, your content or your conduct towards a subscriber.

Nothing limits liability that cannot lawfully be limited.

9. Ending it

You may stop sending at any time and ask for your data to be returned or deleted.

If your account is closed, campaigns already sent remain in the archive unless you ask for them to be removed.

10. Law

These terms are governed by the law of England and Wales. Disputes go to negotiation, then mediation.
