What every plan includes

  • No surprise invoice. Sending stops at the limit rather than carrying on and billing you for it. You cannot overspend here, because there is no overage to charge.
  • Hold the whole trade list. You pay for what you send, not for names sitting in a database. The subscribers limit is there so that contacts you mail twice a year can stay on your list.
  • No daily cap. Send your whole list in one go on the first day if that is what you need.
  • Your own sending domain on every paid plan. Not an add-on, and it will not become one. A sender on their own domain carries their own delivery record, so charging to unlock it would be against everyone's interest.
  • Proof that people agreed, kept for you. Every list carries where the names came from, the wording people agreed to and the date. A signup form records it automatically. That is the question you cannot answer a year later from memory.
  • A permanent page for every campaign. Each one has an address that keeps working, so you can link it, share it and be found through it.
  • Easy to leave. Every email carries an unsubscribe link that works in one click, so a reader who wants less can stop instead of reporting you as spam.

You are judged on what your campaigns did, not on how new your account is.

What you can create

Everything is self-serve. There is nothing to ask ABTA for.

  • More than 300 starter layouts, grouped by what you want to send, or a blank page.
  • Twelve block types, including coloured panels you drag other blocks into, two columns and an image beside text, which stack on a phone.
  • Your own images, uploaded and stored for you. Every one needs a description before it can be used.
  • Your logo, colours and footer saved once on the brand page, and applied to every campaign after that.
  • Blocks you build once and reuse, such as a sign-off.
  • Previews for desktop, phone, Outlook, dark mode and plain text, and a test send to your own team.
  • Every save kept, so you can go back to an earlier draft.
  • A link that shows a draft to a colleague for sign-off, without giving them an account.
  • Scheduling, in your own timezone. A scheduled campaign is locked until you unschedule it, and every check runs again at the moment it goes rather than when you set it.

One thing is deliberately missing: a countdown timer, because it breaks in several mail apps and needs an image server running behind it. One limit applies to fonts: the 30 Google fonts on offer show in mail apps that can load them, but Gmail and some versions of Outlook cannot, so readers there see a similar standard font instead.

Where replies go

Replies to your campaigns come to you, never to ABTA. You can only use an address you have confirmed with a code, so nobody can point replies at an inbox they do not control. The address you sign in with is confirmed already.

Rules for every email you send

  • The list is your own, and you can say how and when each address was collected.
  • No bought, scraped, appended or swapped addresses, ever.
  • Your organisation name appears in the email, with your postal address if you have added one.
  • Unsubscribe works in one click and is honoured immediately.
  • Every image has a description, and the email reads properly with images off.
  • No misleading subject lines, and no content that would breach the acceptable use policy.

What we check before you send

Every campaign is checked automatically, in the second it is sent. Nobody approves it first.

  • Held, and looked at by a person: the campaign asks readers for money or bank details, or an image has no description.
  • Flagged, and sent anyway: a subject line in capitals or full of exclamation marks, or an email that is nearly all images.
  • Checked at import: disposable addresses are dropped, and a list with many role addresses such as info@ or a high share of invalid rows is flagged for us to look at.

Held campaigns are looked at within one working day, and you are told what to change.

What can get an account stopped

Spam reports

0.2%

Above this on a campaign with at least two reports, sending stops automatically. A warning shows from 0.1%.

Addresses that do not exist

5%

Keep bounces under 5%. A higher rate is usually a sign of an old or bought list.

Rule breaches

1

Asking a reader for money, or mailing a list you cannot show people agreed to join.

Suspension is not permanent. Tell us what went wrong and what you have changed, and ABTA can reinstate the account.

ABTA applies these limits to protect delivery. Gmail and Yahoo ask senders to keep spam reports below 0.3% of email, and Google recommends staying under 0.1%, so ABTA warns at 0.1% and stops at 0.2%, before a campaign reaches the level where mail stops arriving, wherever it is sent from.
